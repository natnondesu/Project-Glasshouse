# M6: Network topology and VLANs (2026-10-15)

Builds on M5 port mapping. Each phase merges to `main` when the full gate passes.

## Phases

1. **VLANs**
   - A room keeps a VLAN list: VLAN ID (1–4094, unique) and name. Schema v7 adds `vlans` to each revision.
   - A port may be **access** (one VLAN) or **trunk** (allowed VLANs or all, plus an optional native VLAN). Unset means not configured.
   - Changing a VLAN ID carries through to ports; removing a VLAN takes it off every port.
   - Checks (warnings only):
     - Access ports on both ends of a cable in different VLANs.
     - Access to trunk where the trunk's native VLAN isn't the access VLAN.
     - Trunk to trunk with different allowed VLANs or different native VLANs.
   - Port manager: mode, VLAN, allowed list and native VLAN for the selected port.
   - The cable schedule (sheet and CSV) gains a VLAN column.
2. **Topology**
   - A new **Network** tab: a diagram generated from the room's cables, plus the room's VLAN list.
   - Layout: devices with a single cable hang under the device they connect to; everything else (switches, routers) sits in rows. If one switch aggregates others, it goes on a row above them. Several cables between the same pair draw as one line with a count.
   - Copper and fibre lines look different; picking a VLAN highlights the cables that carry it and dims the rest.
   - Clicking a device shows its ports and VLANs, with buttons to open the port manager or the elevation.
   - Devices with ports but no cables are listed beside the diagram.
   - The Install sheet gets a Network page: the topology diagram plus the VLAN list.

Everything new is in English and Thai.
