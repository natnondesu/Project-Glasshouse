# Glasshouse — Design System

Status: **glass shell over paper-and-ink drawings**, as shipped 2026-10-15. The app shell is "glass and depth" (adopted 2026-10-14); the drawing canvases (plan, Install sheet, network diagram) use the technical-drawing look the product owner asked for afterwards. Exact values live in `apps/web/src/styles/tokens.css`.

## World

**Frosted glass floating over working drawings.** The title block, project tree, view tabs, tools, inspector, checks and side panels are frosted glass on a cool grey-blue ground with a faint construction grid. The drawings they serve — the room plan, the rack elevations, the network diagram and every Install sheet page — are paper and ink: hatched clearance zones, ink outlines, dimension lines and callouts. Glass is the tool; paper is the record.

Light only, chosen for laptops in bright server rooms; there is no theme toggle. The interface works in English and Thai.

Colour never works alone. Blocking issues use a solid circle icon, warnings a dashed circle with "?", equipment state a drawn icon (dashed circle planned, check deployed, triangle in maintenance), cables a solid (copper) or dashed (fibre) line.

## Glass

`.glass` is the only glass recipe: `rgb(247 249 251 / 0.56)` with `backdrop-filter: blur(13px) saturate(1.5)`, a 1px white border at 75% and a 16px radius, with an inset top highlight and a long soft drop. Glass that floats over a drawing (the project drawer) is denser (`--glass-bg-float`, 80%) and adds the float shadow, so linework behind it never smudges its text. Controls inside are translucent white (`--control-bg`) with 8–10px radii; segmented controls use a sunken track with a raised selected item.

## Type

- **Geist Sans** for UI; **Geist Mono** for figures, IDs, units, port names and measurements. **IBM Plex Sans Thai** (Thai subset) sits behind both for Thai text.
- Scale tokens: `--text-2xs` 11 · `--text-xs` 12 · `--text-sm` 13 · `--text-md` 14 · `--text-lg` 16 · `--text-xl` 20; display headings above that are set per screen.
- **11px is the floor** for anything meaningful. The printed sheet uses `--text-print` (12px); its 13-column BOM prints at the 11px floor.
- Drawings keep text at full size when they change scale: the rack elevation fits whole racks by shrinking the unit height (13–16px per U), never the type; the network diagram never fits below 85%.

## Focus and states

- One focus treatment: a solid 2px accent ring (`--focus-ring`, or `--focus-ring-inset` where an outline would be clipped). SVG items show focus by thickening their own line in accent.
- Freezing actions confirm in place: Submit and Approve show what will freeze and the check status before they act. A disabled Approve says what it waits for.
- A frozen revision is said once, in the tab bar: a chip ("R01 Approved · read-only") beside Start new draft, never a banner over each view.
- Checks counts what holds approval the way the Review badge does: blocks and to acknowledge; plain warnings get a quieter chip. Notes on the revision on screen show as a quiet link to Review ("2 notes"), never as a hold. Its head stays one line.
- Review notes are revision notes: written on a revision (approved ones too), read by anyone who opens it later, with an optional unverified name and the time. No resolving; they never hold approval. Earlier revisions' notes fold away under the current one.
- Revisions and history shows the latest 5 revisions (Show all for the rest) and pages events 20 at a time, newest first, with Newer / Older.
- Frozen revisions read as a record, not a form: values sit as plain figures beside their units, remove buttons are hidden, and the panel says Read-only with a lock instead of "Changes save as you type".
- Approval is a stamp: APPROVED, revision, date and approver in a ruled green box, with the Install sheet as the next step.

## Plan drawing

- Walls are heavy rounded ink; objects are ink outlines on paper with mono labels; a Rack's Front is a heavier band with an ink tick.
- Clearance zones are hatched; blocking objects are filled red with a BLOCKING callout.
- Dimensions are drafting style (extension lines, 45° ticks). Power feeds are dashed coloured runs tagged A/B.
- Wall-mounted racks are drawn dashed, as overhead items are; their label adds 'wall'.
- Doors: a swing door draws its leaf open at 90° and the quarter-circle swing; a sliding door draws its track, the leaf parked beside the opening and an arrow for the direction, with no swing.
- Partitions are solid ink walls with doors cut through them; a door standing in one draws its swing or track on the side it opens to, and the wall never shows through it. The rack-front band is a thin line and the partition a solid block, so the legend keeps the two apart.
- Air conditioning and the display wall are drawn as overhead items, dashed like a wall rack: a wall split AC has its vent and three airflow arrows, a precision AC a pinwheel fan and a heavy front band, a display wall tile joints and a heavy screen face. The space to keep in front of each (airflow 1.00 m, service 1.00 m, viewing 1.50 m) is hatched like a clearance zone while the unit is selected or while a rule about it has fired (then amber, the same colour as the warning). The zone's name and depth sit near its far edge, in a layer above the distance lines so the label's paper halo cuts a line instead of the line running through the words.
- An MCB panel the survey drew but the room won't use is drawn grey and dashed (still named on hover, and "not in use" to screen readers); a blocking flag still turns it red.
- The legend is on demand from the zoom bar, never left over the drawing.
- Below 1360px the inspector starts collapsed so the drawing gets the room (Plan, Elevation and Network alike); once someone opens or closes it, that choice is remembered, and selecting something new reopens it.
- Names and figures are never cut: a rack name too wide for the rack sits under it, and a fill count that doesn't fit is left out.
- A selected node fills its slot in accent with a heavy outline; its chassis gets a dashed accent outline.
- ⌘/Ctrl+C and V copy a plan object (a rack with its devices). The copy takes the next free name (Neopolis-01 → Neopolis-05) and lands beside the original, else on the nearest spot clear of objects, cut-outs and clearance zones; pasting again builds a row. Cables, feeds and on-site state stay with the original.

## Elevation

- A switch joins a stack (or starts one, named after itself without the trailing number) from its inspector: member number 1–9, the other members as links, Leave stack. Members wear an "S1" tag before their name in the rack, on screen and on paper. Only network gear in a rack can stack; a switch is in one stack at most.

- Racks read in plan order: rows from the top of the drawing down, each left to right. Drag a rack by its name (or Alt+←/→) to reorder; the order is kept on the room beside the revisions, so it holds on an approved plan, travels in the file and orders the Install sheet. "Reset to plan order" appears once there is one.

## Network diagram

- Generated from the cables, never hand-drawn. Switch-like gear (Network category, 4+ ports) forms the rows; single-cable devices hang under their switch in wrapped rows of up to six (fewer on the printed page).
- A core pair joined by a bundle sits side by side over the switches they share. Diagram labels are 13px so the 85% fit floor keeps them at 11px.
- Parallel cables draw as one line with a count (named after the LAG when they form one); copper solid, fibre dashed accent. Highlighting a VLAN fades what doesn't carry it.
- Every box in a diagram is one size, grown to the longest name; past 26 characters a name wraps at a hyphen or space, never cut. Leaves wrap to the canvas's shape, so a big room is a block that scrolls a little both ways, not a strip many screens wide.
- A port with no VLAN of its own carries the VLAN of the port at the other end of its cable: shown with a dashed underline and "from SW port", and counted when a VLAN is highlighted. Nothing is copied; setting this end overrides it.
- A switch stack is one box: "STACK name", a line per member ("1  ICON-CS-B10-01"), and its stack ring (cables between members) as a count, drawn as two offset outlines. Cables to any member reach the box; only the stack's row grows taller. Any member in maintenance marks the stack. Selecting it lists each member with its own ports, and its port tags start with the member ("S2 GE1/0/3 ⇄ eth0"), since members often share port names. Selecting a device cabled to a stack names the member at the far end the same way ("eth0 ⇄ S2 GE1/0/3", and "to DIST-02 S2 Te1/0/1" in its port list). A port-channel belongs to the whole stack, so a LAG name never takes a member: a LAG across both members stays one line ("Po100 ⇄ Po101 · 2 links").
- The Network bar has the room search: picking a device selects its box (its stack's, or its chassis's), scrolls to it and rings it for a moment; a rack goes to its first device in the diagram; a device without cables says it's under Not cabled.
- Gear in maintenance: burnt-orange box with an "In maintenance" tab, its cables orange. In the port panel, a jack whose far end is in maintenance gets an orange ring and the triangle, and the link line says so.

## 3D

- Labels are sprites at a fixed screen size; the Labels menu picks which kinds are always labelled, the rest label on hover.
- Partitions stand full height (2.40 m by default) with a doorway cut wherever a door is in them and the wall carried on above it. A wall split AC hangs on the wall as a white case with a louvre slot and a status light; a precision AC is a floor cabinet with a display, an air grille and a plinth; a display wall is a dark bezel with the screen set into it and the joints between its tiles, at its stored height. Walking passes under a unit hung above head height (a split AC) and through a partition's doorway, and is stopped by everything else.
- Walk puts you in the room at eye height (1.6 m, a wider 65° view), first asking where to start, like Street View: a pin follows the pointer on the floor (green where you can stand, red where you can't) and a click drops you there facing into the room; a spot too close to a rack moves to the nearest free floor. Start at the door is one click; Esc or Cancel goes back. Click the floor to glide there; drag to look; W/S or ↑/↓ walk, A/D step aside, ←/→ turn, Shift hurries; two-finger scroll walks too. Walls, racks, other objects and columns stop you, sliding along them. The rack list (and search) stands you in front of a rack's Front, looking at it. Esc or Back to overview returns to the orbit view; clicking a rack still selects it.
- MCB panels show their breakers on the front face in the panel's arrangement (levers up on ways with a feed; instanced meshes, so 72 ways stay light). A panel not in use is a neutral grey cabinet with grey breakers; its object card says "Not in use".
- A highlighted power path glows and carries arrowheads moving from source to load (still under reduced motion); everything else on the power layer fades.

## Catalog

- Its own page: the template list (each with its port mix) beside a wide editor whose top is the template's front panel, drawn as placed devices will show it and redrawn as you edit.
- Each port group sets its layout: one row, or two rows with odd numbers on top (how most switches number) or left to right. Groups draw as labelled blocks; jacks come in modules of six columns that wrap to fit.
- Duplicate starts a variant from a template; the editor says how many placed devices use it (they keep the version they were placed from).

## MCB panels

- The panel manager draws the panel face as a paper-and-ink drawing of the enclosure: the main breaker on top when one is set, then a tile per way in the chosen layout (one row, one column, two columns; two columns number odd-left/even-right or down-left-then-right). A tile carries the way number, rating, phase, its feed and label; its toggle sits up when the way carries a feed and down when it is spare. `wayPosition` in the domain places every way, so the manager, the 3D face and the Install sheet agree.
- Clicking (or arrow-keying to) a tile picks it; its rating, label and feed are edited in a strip under the face. The face scrolls inside the dialog (a one-row 48-way panel scrolls sideways), so a 72-way panel never leaves the window.
- "In use" is a switch, on by default. Off draws the face dashed and grey and lists the feeds still wired to it; new feeds can't pick it (shown disabled with "(not in use)") and a feed still on it blocks approval.

## Port panel

- The faceplate follows the template's layout (the catalog's current version, matched by port name, so a layout set later reaches placed gear). A port removed on a device leaves its place empty; ports a device gained sit in blocks of their own.

- Stays inside the screen wherever it's dragged; below the faceplate it scrolls (a div: a fieldset won't scroll), with edge shadows while there's more. A grip in the header and a "Drag to move" tip say it moves; the panel manager works the same way.
- Link to device is a type-to-filter list (any words of the device or rack name), each option with its rack and free-port count.
- Shift-click picks a range of jacks, ⌘/Ctrl-click adds one. Several picked show a group form: every field starts on "Leave as is" (with the shared value, or "mixed"), and Apply changes only what was filled in, in one undo step. A LAG's members outside the selection are named before their shared VLAN changes.

## Uplinks between rooms

- A port can be an uplink to a port in another room of the same site instead of a cable in its room: Link to another room… picks the room, the device (same search) and a free port there, and by default sets the far end to point back, while that room is a draft. Each room keeps its own end and approves on its own.
- The jack gets a dashed accent ring; the port reads "uplink to IDF-SW Gi1 in IDF" with whether the far end points back. Unlink clears both ends while the far room is a draft.
- Checks warn (never block) when the far end doesn't point back, no longer exists, or is cabled inside its room.
- The network diagram draws the far device as a dashed box "In IDF", on a dotted accent line; it links to that room's network. Cable schedule and CSV list uplinks with the far room as the rack.

## Install sheet

- Cable schedule: seven columns, each end one cell (device over its rack), so 70 cables fit A4 portrait.
- The BOM prints on A4 landscape with fixed column shares; names wrap at hyphens.
- Panel schedules: one boxed schedule for each MCB panel in use that has feeds, drawn in its arrangement (main breaker on top when set; way, rating and phase, rack and feed per cell; spare ways dashed), ink only so black-and-white prints read the same. A one-row panel wraps after 12 breakers on paper. Panels not in use are listed as "Not in use" with no schedule.
- The network page prints the full diagram on portrait, else on landscape; a room too big for either prints its network gear only, each switch counting the devices on it (the cable schedule lists them). Never below the 11px floor.

## Home

- The project tree's header (Projects and its actions) and the backup panel stay put; only the tree scrolls between them.
- Server mode (shared server, P1): the title block says "Connected to the Glasshouse server" / "Saved to the server · time"; the browser-backup panel is hidden. On room screens the status shortens so the breadcrumb keeps the names: a cloud with "Connected", a green check with "Saved 10:37 PM" (or "In this browser" before the first save), the full sentence in its hint and for screen readers. A failed save stays in full, in red, as an alert; a lost connection keeps its banner. When the connection is lost (no events or heartbeat for 35 s, or the stream closes) the app goes read-only after 3 s with the banner "Lost the connection… Edits you already made are kept and will be sent", and recovers by itself. An edit the server refuses shows a closable notice "A change didn't go through: <reason>"; closing the tab while edits are unsent asks first.
- Catalog lives at the far right of the title block, past a divider, on every page (icon only below 1360 px wide, as is Export project file); that corner is where user settings join later.
- The title block is always one line: names truncate with an ellipsis (the full name in their hint) instead of wrapping or overlapping, at 1280 px in English and Thai. Names win over status text: a typical Project › Site › Room reads in full at 1280 and 1440 in both languages, so truncation is for long names only.
- Home, the catalog and the users page keep the project tree docked, with its collapse-to-rail. Home reads top to bottom: the workspace bar, the globe the full width of the page, then the numbers and the rooms of whatever the globe has in scope.
- The workspace bar (glass, one row): "Workspace" (the page heading) over the date and "2 projects · 3 sites · 7 rooms", then a strip for the room this browser opened last: its plan in miniature (floor and racks, blocking ones red, no names), "Pick up where you left off" as the strip's own label in normal case (never an eyebrow), "Server Room · Riverside Campus — edited by Ploy 2 hours ago · R02 draft", its holds ("2 blocking" with the solid circle, else "to acknowledge" with the warning icon) and Continue. "Edited by" is the shared server's: the latest change to the room by someone signed in, from the command log (served with the workspace and on each command event; the dev stand-in names nobody); in one browser it is left out. Any command addressed to the room counts as an edit: plan and survey changes, rename, undo, review steps (submit, approve, notes, acknowledgements), equipment state, rack order, archive and restore, and creating it. The relative time moves on each minute. With no last room (or one that is gone) the strip offers the first room something holds ("Needs attention next"), else it hides. No create actions in the bar: the tree has them.
- Home's search sits in the title block, on Home only (elsewhere the title block shows the workspace's counts): "Search sites and rooms" matches project, site and room names, every word, ignoring case and Latin accents, and Thai tone marks (่ ้ ๊ ๋ ์) but never Thai vowels. `/` or ⌘K focuses it (the key shows in the field), Escape clears it. Typing fades the pins it doesn't keep (counts say "1 of 3 match"; a faded pin's name ends "no match" for screen readers), and narrows the numbers and the rooms; a hidden status line announces what is left ("3 rooms in 1 site") once typing, scope or chips settle; the globe never moves on a keystroke: "Show 1 match" under the crumbs fits them. Enter opens the top room match, or picks and flies to the site when the site's name matches best.
- The globe sets the scope: World (every site, placed or not; "1 not on the map yet, counted here"), a country or province (its count, when it holds all of that place's sites, or its crumb), a count's own sites, or one site (its pin, Enter on a site match, a saved location). Home opens on World, framed on where the sites are. The breadcrumb's solid step is the scope; dragging or zooming never changes it. The scope is hatched on the map. Search, scope and room chip live in the address (`?q=`, `?scope=country:TH`, `?rooms=draft`), so a filtered Home can be shared or reloaded: typing settles 150 ms before the address follows, a scope or chip change is a history step (Back restores the previous view), and an address Home didn't write (the logo's plain "/", a pasted link) is read back into the view; a scope whose sites are gone falls back to the world, and a region scope opens out to the world when the map can't load.
- A picked site's card floats over the map's top right, beside the zoom (dense glass with the float shadow, 360 px, scrolling inside when the map is short), closable (its button, or Escape), closing steps the scope up to the site's province, else its country, and focus goes to the site's pin. On a short map the 3D gives way so the facts and the first three rooms stay in view; when only a sliver would be left it folds into a strip (the room's name, prev/next, "Show the room in 3D"), and the card's foot fades while there is more to scroll to. The camera keeps the picked site in the part of the map the card leaves free. The card: the site's name, its project, where it is ("13.66800° N, 100.60500° E" with Edit, or "Not on the map yet" with Place on the map), then rooms / racks / power planned against the rooms' limits (unknown draws counted, never zero). The picked room (the last one opened, when it is here) is live in 3D on paper (the 3D rules below), named in its corner chip, with prev/next when the site has several rooms. Over its foot sits the room's record as the Review stamp reads it (state, then revision · date · designer or approver, "(unverified)" for typed names); on hover or focus the rack names take its place. Under it one row per room: name, revision chip, racks and planned power, and what holds approval in the Review badge's terms as links to Review (or "Install sheet ready", or "Nothing holds approval"). Rooms change by a click on their row or prev/next, never on a timer. A site with no rooms says so and offers New room. The card steps aside in Place mode.
- When the picked room has holds, the way to them leads: "Resolve 2 items" (to Review) is the primary button and "Open <room>" steps aside; otherwise "Open <room>" leads. Once a revision is approved the Install sheet is a quiet icon button at the row's end (its name is its hint). The actions never wrap: a long room name gives way with an ellipsis. No small label above the site name; "Last opened" sits on the room's row.
- The 3D turns slowly (about one turn a minute). It stops under the pointer so a drag looks around from there, has a pause button, holds still under reduced motion, stops drawing when scrolled away, and never takes the scroll wheel. Clicking a rack opens it in the elevation. The scene draws frames only while turning or when something changes. If the 3D fails to load, its panel says so and the rest of Home stays.
- The globe is an orthographic map in the drawing world: a pale sea, paper land, ink country borders, Thailand's provinces dashed, a graticule whose step follows the zoom (15° on the world view down to hundredths of a degree), and the scope's region (the picked site's country, or closer in its province) hatched with a heavy outline; up close only the outline stays. From country zoom it adds water and places: lakes as light water with a fine shore, rivers as thin accent-tinted blue lines; city dots (capitals ringed, the drafting convention) named by importance (capitals and the biggest cities on a country's opening frame, more closer in, every town at city zoom), and an authored aircraft symbol for airports, named at city zoom. Names of countries (small caps), provinces, cities and airports are drawn where they fit, beside their dot, in the interface language (every place, airport and country is named in Thai in Thai: checked tables first, then Wikidata's Thai label for each Natural Earth feature, committed with the data; the few towns with no Thai name anywhere are left off the map in both languages), never under a pin or its name, the crumbs, the zoom, the card or another name; a province named like the city already labelled is said once. A drafting scale bar and "Map data: Natural Earth" (plus "sample sites at illustrative spots" when sample sites are placed) sit bottom left. All of it is Natural Earth (public domain) from one pinned commit: South-East Asia at 1:10m, the rest of the world coarse; served by Glasshouse itself and loaded with the map only on Home.
- It opens framed on the country (or countries) of the placed sites. Pins are ink rings with a dot and the site's name and room count; pins closer than 40 px merge into an ink count ("3") named after the province or country they share; the world view shows one count per country. Every marker is a button ("Thailand — 3 sites. Show them", "Bangna campus, Sample · Northgate DC, 2 rooms"); the selected one has an accent ring and a bold name, never colour alone, and while its site is picked a soft accent ring grows and fades round it every 2.2 s (transform and opacity only; none under reduced motion, where the ring and bold name carry it). Clicking a count flies in to its sites (1.4 s, one ease; the zoom eases in log steps and pulls back on long trips); when no zoom could pull them apart (a campus 50 m wide) a solid list of those sites opens instead, focus on the first, Escape to close. Clicking a site picks it and flies close. A location saved in the dialog picks that site and brings it into view.
- The breadcrumb (top left, solid) runs World › Country › Province › Site down to the scope (with no place picked it follows the middle of the view); the scope's step is drawn solid ink, and every step flies there and becomes the scope. Zoom + and − sit top right. Drag moves the globe under the pointer; when the map has focus the arrow keys move it and + and − zoom. The wheel zooms only with Cmd/Ctrl (or a trackpad pinch), or after the map was clicked (or reached from the keyboard) while the pointer stays over it; leaving the map disarms it. Otherwise the page scrolls and the map says how to zoom ("Hold Ctrl and scroll…", the key written out), briefly. Wheel lines and pages count like pixels, so Firefox zooms the same.
- The world view turns slowly (3° a second, drawn at about 30 frames a second) with a pause button under the zoom ("Stop the globe turning" / "Turn the globe"), and stops on drag, wheel, keys, pointing at the map, focus inside it, Place mode or scrolling away; nothing else moves on its own. Under reduced motion it holds still (no pause button) and flights are jump cuts. The canvas draws only while moving, and again when the screen's pixel ratio changes. Names drawn on the map keep clear of the markers and of the map's own controls.
- Sites without a location wait behind "Not placed yet" (with a count), a button at the map's foot that opens their list above it: each name picks the site, and editors get Place. Escape closes the list back to its button. Nothing drops off Home for want of a location.
- A site's location is set from the tree's row action (a pin) or the card: the Location dialog takes a Google Maps link (place pins, map views, ?q=, ll=, the maps URL API, geo: and OpenStreetMap links) or typed coordinates (decimal, with N/S/E/W, or degrees-minutes-seconds), says what it read as you type ("Reads as 13.66810° N, 100.60520° E"), explains short maps.app.goo.gl links (they can't be read offline), and can take a site off the map. The field takes up to 2,000 characters. Take off the map asks first in the ordinary popup ("Take Bangna campus off the map? You can place it again any time."); No brings the dialog back. Escape closes it. Place on the map instead switches Home's globe to Place mode: focus moves to the map and the instruction is announced; a solid banner over the map's foot says what to do; a click at country zoom zooms in to the province there (clicks wait until that flight lands), a click closer in drops the pin (again to move it, Enter drops it at the crosshair), Save location stores it. Escape (from anywhere) or Cancel leaves; focus goes back to what started it, or to the site's new pin when that is gone. Paste a link instead opens the dialog, as does Place while the map can't load. Editors only, never on archived sites.
- Until the map arrives the card and rooms stand alone with "Loading the map…"; if it can't load its panel says "Map unavailable" and that the site details and rooms still work, with Reload page.
- Under the globe, the scope's numbers: a heading naming the scope ("Thailand · 3 sites", "Bangna campus", "· matching “shaft”" while searching), then one ruled glass strip like a drawing's title block, four cells with figures in the mono face: Rooms (7, "2 approved" in green · "5 draft", in review when any), Racks planned ("77 devices placed"), Power planned in kW with a thin bar against the rooms' limits ("51% of 68.8 kW room limits", red and "over" past them, "No room limits set" without any, and "2 unknown draws not counted" in the warning colour), Before approval ("3 blocking" in the blocking colour with its icon, "2 to acknowledge · 2 rooms"; a check and "Nothing holds approval" when clear). The hold figure is a way there: to the one room's Review, or to the Needs attention rooms below. The cells wrap cleanly on narrow screens.
- The rooms of the scope follow: "All rooms" or "Rooms in Bangna campus" with Show all, and chips All · Needs attention · Draft · Approved with their counts (within scope and search). A dashed card adds a room only when one site is in scope. Nothing left says so honestly ("No rooms match “x” here.") with Clear filters.
- A "Skip to main content" link is the first Tab stop on every page. Outside a room the title block shows the workspace (projects · rooms) instead of blank cells; on Home, Home's search.

## Room navigation

- Rooms open without the project tree: the drawing gets the width. A room that isn't in the workspace (deleted elsewhere, a stale link) is a not-found page and keeps the tree docked.
- The title block carries a breadcrumb, Project › Site › Room, then the revision and its status stamp. The project opens the project drawer where you are, like the Projects button (with the tree pinned it moves focus to the open room in the tree); it never jumps Home — the logo does that. The room, drawn as a control with a caret, opens the switcher; the site opens it on that site's rooms. When space runs out the project name gives way first, then the site; the room stays whole up to half the trail.
- The switcher is a solid popover under the crumb: this project's rooms grouped by site, the current one checked, each with the revision chip the tree shows. Archived rooms and sites stay out, except the room that is open. Past 8 rooms a filter field leads (any words of the room or site name; Enter opens the first match). Switching keeps the view you are in. Keyboard: Enter, Space or ↓ open it, arrows move, Enter opens the room, Escape closes back to the crumb, Tab closes and carries on.
- "Projects" (icon only below 1360 px) opens the full tree as a drawer floating over the left of the room, with focus on the open room. It closes when a room is picked, on Escape, on a click outside or when focus moves on; a picked room and Escape hand focus back to the button. `[` toggles it from anywhere in a room except a text field or dialog; it is matched by key position, so the Thai layout's บ key works too, and the button's hint says so. A room added from the tree keeps the drawer until it is named, then the drawer steps aside.
- The drawer's pin (pressed when on) docks the tree in rooms as before, remembered in this browser; the Projects button then goes, and `[` moves focus into the docked tree. Unpinning leaves it open as a drawer until dismissed. The pin is its own preference: an old "tree open" setting never pins anyone.
- The browser backup panel stays at the bottom of the tree, so in a room it lives in the drawer. While the workspace isn't backed up, the Projects button carries a dot, and its label and hint say "workspace not backed up".
- The inspector keeps its rule (collapsed below 1360 px until opened): at 1280 the plan and network fit with it open, but the sample elevation cuts its third rack in half (all three whole with it folded), so the drawing still gets the room by default.
- Every room is a card: its plan drawn small at its real width so labels keep 11px (blocking objects in red); the room name is the card's link and the hold counts are links of their own, revision chip, its site and project, facts, and its approval holds in the Review badge's terms.

## Sign-in and accounts

- **Sign-in screen**: a glass card centred on the grid ground. "Sign in with your company account" comes first; below an "or" rule sits the email-and-password form. A wrong password is explained in an alert; a first-time local user is taken to "Choose your own password" before anything else.
- **User menu**: lives in the title block's tools corner, after Catalog. The button reads the person's name; the menu shows name, email, a role stamp, Users (admins only) and Sign out.
- **View-only banner**: for Viewers, the app is read-only and the same banner slot that reports a lost connection says "You can view this workspace. Ask an admin for edit access."
- **Users screen** (`/admin/users`, admins only): one glass panel whose header, toolbar and messages hold still while only the list scrolls under a sticky header; fits a 1280 × 720 laptop with no sideways scroll. The toolbar finds a person among hundreds: a search on name and email (`/` or ⌘K, Escape clears), segmented chips for status (Everyone · Active · Switched off · Must change password; Active is the default, so switched-off people stay out of the way), sign-in, role and the access-review questions (Inactive 90+ days · New in 30 days), with counts on the chips that answer review questions and "12 of 340 people" plus Clear filters beneath. The view lives in the address (shareable, survives reload) and the last one, minus the search, is remembered in the browser. Columns sort from their headers (names in the interface language's order). Each row is one height: the name with the email under it in Geist Mono and a quiet Company / Local tag; the role as a select (your own as the user menu's stamp with "You"); the status dot and word with "Last signed in 3 days ago" under it (exact time on hover); and one actions button (drawn dots) opening a solid menu — Rename (inline in the name cell), Reset password (local accounts), Switch off / Switch on, and Delete account last, apart, in red, on switched-off people only. Granting Admin or lowering an admin asks first; every change says what happened in a notice above the list, where errors also appear. Confirmations and person-named labels carry the email, so two people with one name stay apart. "Add local user" in the header (only when password sign-in is on) opens the form in place; a new or reset temporary password appears once, in a box with the code in mono type, and is never shown again.
- **Claim strip**: a slim strip between the view tabs and the drawing, always 44 px high so the drawing never jumps when the holder changes. When someone else holds the room it uses the attention style (warn background, warn left rule) with their name, the time of their last edit and Take over; when it is you it is neutral (control background, accent left rule) with "Done editing".
- **Fingerprint**: the first 12 hex characters of the approved revision's hash, in the mono face and the muted ink, never a second stamp. It sits on the approval stamp in the review panel and in the title block of the Install sheet, so a printed sheet can be matched to what was approved.
- **Claim-lost dialog**: the ordinary centred popup, narrowed to 480 px. It says who took over and how many changes were not saved, with "Download my version" as the primary action and Dismiss beside it. Discarding asks once more, because the copy lives only in that browser.

## Archive

- Admins archive a project, site or room from the tree's row actions, behind the ordinary primary-button confirmation that says what goes with it ("Archive Annex and its 2 rooms? They become read-only and hidden. You can restore them any time."). Archived work can't be deleted until it is restored (the delete button says so). Importing a file never archives or restores anything: a merge drops the file's archive stamps and can't reach into archived parts, and a copy starts active. Restoring needs the parent restored first: the row's Restore reads "Restore Site Annex first." and says so when pressed.
- Archived items leave the tree, the home page, the hero and the title block's counts. "Show archived" (with how many) sits under the tree, only once something is archived, and is kept in this browser; archived rows then read in the quiet ink with a dashed, hatched "Archived" stamp. Only the item that was archived wears the stamp; what is archived with it is greyed. The open room and the way to it never hide.
- An archived room opens with one dashed neutral strip under the view tabs: "Archived by Ada on Oct 18, 2026. Read-only." with Restore for admins (or "Archived with site Annex … Restore Site Annex first."). Grey, not amber: it is filed, not blocked. Nothing is added to the drawing itself. Controls are disabled the way an approved revision's are, without Start new draft; the Install sheet still opens.

## Prohibitions

- No dark mode until it's designed deliberately.
- No glass without content behind it, and no glass on the drawings themselves.
- No gradient text or decorative glow.
- No colour as the only signal; no Unicode characters standing in for icons.
- No meaningful text below 11px, and no shrinking a drawing's text to make it fit.
- The page-wide construction grid behind the glass is intentional; it is what the glass refracts.
